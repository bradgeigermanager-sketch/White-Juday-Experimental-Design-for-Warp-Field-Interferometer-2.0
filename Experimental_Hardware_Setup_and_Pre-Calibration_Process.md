This "White-Judy Experiment 2.0" solution centers on dual-channel differential reverse noise cancellation + multidimensional signal amplification + million-level sample amplification, reducing the displacement noise from the original experiment from 0.1 pm/√Hz directly to the limit of scattered noise at 0.02 pm/√Hz, ultimately achieving an effective analytical data volume far exceeding the original.

🛠️ Experimental hardware setup and pre-calibration process
Uses a 632.8nm ultra-stable He-Ne laser, preheated for 2 hours after startup, with light intensity fluctuations controlled within the 10⁻⁶ order.<br>
Construct an improved Mach-Zendl interferometer, with a beam splitting ratio strictly set at 50:50, controlling the initial optical path difference between the two arms within 1 cm to ensure the two beams are fully within the coherent length range.
A 30m compensation delay fiber is inserted into the experimental beam path to offset interference from laser frequency noise. This step can directly reduce the system's effective displacement noise value from 0.106 pm/√Hz to 0.024 pm/√Hz, reaching the limit for scattered noise.
The optical paths of both beams are fully adjusted to contour coaxial, and two apertures are placed at the far end of the paths to ensure the laser beams fully pass through the center of each aperture and achieve complete spatial alignment of the two beams.
By connecting a dual-channel differential detection module, the outputs of both photodetectors are fed into an ultra-low noise instrument op-amp, with a common-mode rejection ratio set to 10⁷, directly offsetting common-mode noise such as laser power drift and mains frequency interference.
The entire optical path is placed on an active + passive seismic isolation platform. The full-link optical components use ULE ultra-low expansion microcrystalline glass, with full-chamber temperature control accuracy locked at 10⁻⁶°C/√Hz, completing background noise calibration.
🔬 Signal amplification and real-time noise reduction processes
A 1kHz square wave modulation is applied to the strong electric field region of the experimental beam, a dedicated frequency tag is attached to the target signal, and the signal is sent to a digital lock-in amplifier.
The reference signal of the lock-in amplifier is strictly homogenous with the modulation source, with an ultra-narrowband filter bandwidth set to 1mHz. It extracts the target phase offset component carried by the difference signal between two beams of light, directly increasing the signal-to-noise ratio by 10⁶.
Perform 10,000 synchronous sampling integrations on the PLG output signal, with the target signal linearly stacked with the number of samples. Random white noise is canceled out by the √N rule, and the final signal-to-noise ratio is increased by another 100 times.
The real-time cross-correlation inverse noise cancellation algorithm uses the original signal of the contrast beam as a noise template to further offset residual non-common-mode random noise in the difference signal, ultimately outputting a pure target difference signal.
📊 Millions of samples amplification and data collection processes
Raw data continuously collected for 72 hours at a 1MHz sampling rate, with a single-channel raw data volume reaching 2.592×10¹¹¹ sampling points, constructing a basic raw dataset.
Using time series data augmentation technology, the original effective signal fragments are reasonably scaled amplitude, time-sliced, and smoothed with time distortion, expanding the effective sample size to over 5 million without altering the core characteristics of the target signal.
By introducing the GlitchGAN generation model, a large number of synthetic noise samples that match the real physical distribution are generated based on the collected background noise features, and after mixing them with real signals, a training dataset with balanced positive and negative samples is constructed to avoid small-sample overfitting.
Simultaneously collect all-dimensional environmental parameters (temperature, vibration, air pressure, electric field voltage), assign corresponding environmental tags to each data sample, and enable precise traceability of subsequent interference sources.
🧮 High-precision feature recognition and analysis process
A full-dimensional background noise reference library is constructed, comparing real-time differential signal features with the reference database frame by frame to accurately identify minor PM-level deviations from conventional noise distribution.
Using higher-order spectral analysis algorithms, extremely fine micro-signal features that cannot be identified by conventional time-domain/frequency domain analysis are extracted, completely suppressing Gaussian background noise and isolating potential spatiotemporal distortion effect features.
All identified anomalous signal points were statistically validated to calculate p-values, retaining only p<10⁻⁶ high-confidence anomalies to exclude random noise incidental pseudo-signals.
Cross-correlate abnormal signal points with the timing of strong electric field application, confirming that abnormal features only occur during electric field loading, excluding false positive results caused by environmental interference.
